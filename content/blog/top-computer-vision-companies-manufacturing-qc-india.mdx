# Top Computer Vision Companies for Manufacturing QC in India (2026)

Search for this list and you will find a dozen versions of it, almost all published by companies that rank themselves first. That tells you something useful about the market and nothing useful about who to hire.

We build computer vision systems, so we are on this list too. We have put ourselves where we actually belong — in the custom-build group, after a firm with twenty-three years and five hundred projects behind it. What follows is organised the way a plant manager actually decides: not by rank, but by what you are buying.

## What are you actually choosing between?

Indian manufacturers evaluating computer vision are usually comparing three categories of vendor that are not substitutes for each other, and most disappointing projects come from picking the wrong category rather than the wrong company.

**Global sensor vendors** sell configurable hardware and software for standard inspections. **CCTV analytics platforms** read your existing camera feeds for process, safety or grading use cases. **Custom builders** train models for defects no catalogue product recognises. A sensor vendor cannot solve a novel defect. A custom builder is overkill for barcode verification. A CCTV platform cannot inspect at line speed.

Get the category right and the vendor choice inside it is usually straightforward.

## Global sensor vendors: Cognex, Keyence, Omron

These are the default and often the correct answer. They are engineered products with warranties, local support and a decade of field history. If your inspection is standard, buying one of these is the lower-risk decision, and any honest vision consultancy will tell you so.

### Cognex

The stronger choice for complex inspection. Cognex's deep-learning tooling is generally selected when an application involves multiple measurements or defect types that rule-based vision handles poorly. Buyers comparing the two tend to land on Cognex as application complexity rises. Published figures put equivalent DM300-class readers in the $3,500–5,000 range, higher than the Keyence equivalent.

**Best for:** complex, multi-measurement inspection where you want a supported product rather than a project.
**Consider otherwise if:** your defect is genuinely unusual, or the licensing and hardware cost is disproportionate to one inspection point.

### Keyence

Known for a direct sales force and in-plant demonstrations — they will bring the unit to your line and run it on your product, which is a real advantage when you are unsure whether vision will work at all. Positioned as an all-in-one system. Basic 2D Ethernet readers start around $2,500–3,500.

**Best for:** pharmaceuticals, electronics, precision engineering and packaging where flexible standard inspection is required, and for buyers who want to see it working before committing.
**Consider otherwise if:** application complexity is high — that is where Cognex tends to win.

### Omron

The FH series runs AI defect inspection on the controller itself, which removes the need for a separate GPU server for lighter deep-learning workloads, and trains from relatively few images. Its capability is narrower than dedicated deep-learning tooling but covers a lot of surface inspection.

**Best for:** plants already standardised on Omron automation — it drops into that ecosystem cleanly.
**Consider otherwise if:** you run another PLC brand, where it is competitive but not clearly differentiated.

## CCTV analytics platforms: Wobot, Assert AI, Detect Technologies, Intello Labs

This group is frequently listed alongside line-inspection vendors, and that comparison misleads buyers. These platforms read **existing** cameras, typically at a few frames per second, for process and safety outcomes. That is a genuinely valuable category — it is just not packaging-line QC.

### Wobot.ai

Turns existing CCTV into a process compliance tool: checklist adherence, hygiene and SOP monitoring, task verification, sold as SaaS. Well established in food service and retail chains, where consistent execution across many outlets is the entire problem.

**Best for:** multi-site operations where the question is "is the process being followed?"

### Assert AI

Founded in 2019 by Job Philip and Nitin Jain, Mumbai-based. Integrates with existing CCTV networks for real-time operational insight, with alerting for intrusion, fire and smoke, PPE violations and material-handling irregularities. Its Pratham platform lets users work with video data without writing code.

**Best for:** enterprise-scale monitoring across manufacturing, warehousing and logistics sites.

### Detect Technologies

Incorporated in February 2016 and incubated at IIT Madras. Focused on heavy industry — asset integrity, worker safety, HSSE and resource monitoring across plants, refineries and construction, combining video analytics with UAVs, ultrasonic and thermography, and IIoT sensing.

**Best for:** refineries, heavy plants and large sites where the risk being managed is safety and asset failure, not product defects.
**Note:** this is deliberately a different problem from production-line QC. Listing it as a QC vendor, as some roundups do, misrepresents what they do well.

### Intello Labs

Applies vision to objective quality grading of fruits, vegetables and commodities from photographs, replacing subjective manual grading in agricultural supply chains.

**Best for:** agri supply chains where standardised grading changes pricing, disputes and waste economics.

## Custom builders: Kritikal Solutions, Buteforce

You end up here when the first two groups have failed you — typically because the defect is specific to your product, or because it needs to be caught at a speed and in a lighting environment no catalogue product was designed for.

### Kritikal Solutions

The most established independent option in this group. A design house with 23+ years of history and over 500 projects, spanning computer vision, OCR, embedded systems and IoT, serving manufacturing, MedTech, automotive, FMCG, drones and traffic surveillance.

**Best for:** buyers who want custom work with the risk profile of a long-established firm, or who need embedded and hardware engineering alongside the vision model.

### Buteforce

Ours. [Chennai-based](/ai-automation-company-in-chennai), founded 2025, focused narrowly on production-grade vision, document AI and automation rather than a broad services catalogue. Our [manufacturing QC deployment](/work/ortho-classifier) — a YOLOv8 model trained on 10,000+ product images on an orthopaedic insole line — holds **99.2% classification accuracy at 120 items per minute** and cut inspection errors **94% in its first month**. We work to fixed price and fixed timeline, typically 2–8 weeks, and the client owns the trained model and the code.

**Best for:** a specific, well-defined defect problem where you want a working system quickly and want to own it afterwards.
**Consider otherwise if:** you need a nationwide support contract, 24/7 SLAs, or embedded hardware design — Kritikal or a sensor vendor is the better fit. We are a small team and say so.

## How should you actually choose?

Work down this list and stop at the first yes.

1. **Is the inspection standard — presence, absence, barcode, dimensional measurement?** Buy Cognex, Keyence or Omron. A custom model adds cost and risk for no benefit.
2. **Is the question "is the process being followed?" rather than "is this unit defective?"** You want CCTV analytics — Wobot or Assert AI — not a line inspection system.
3. **Is it safety, asset integrity or site monitoring at a heavy plant?** Detect Technologies is built for that.
4. **Are you grading agricultural produce?** Intello Labs.
5. **Is the defect specific to your product, at your line speed, in your lighting — and did a sensor vendor already tell you it won't work?** Now you need a custom build. Then choose between an established design house and a small specialist on the basis of scope, support needs and ownership terms.

## Not a fit if…

A custom vision system is the wrong purchase in several common situations, and it is worth being direct about them.

If your line runs below roughly 20 units a minute and a person is already catching defects reliably, the payback is poor — automate something else first. If you cannot supply several hundred images of the actual defect, nobody can train a model for you yet, and any vendor who says otherwise is selling a pilot. If your reject mechanism does not exist or cannot be synchronised to the PLC, you will get a system that detects defects and passes them anyway, which is worse than no system.

If your inspection is legally regulated by net content, a checkweigher measures mass and is the defensible instrument — vision complements it, it does not replace it. And if nobody on your side owns the project after go-live, it will drift out of calibration within a quarter regardless of who built it.

## Where to start

Photograph fifty defective units and fifty good ones on your own line, in your own lighting, on a phone. Send that to any vendor on this list — including the sensor vendors — and ask them to tell you honestly whether their product handles it. The ones who answer with a per-defect assessment rather than a demo reel are the ones worth talking to further.

If it turns out to be a standard inspection, buy the sensor. That is genuinely the right answer more often than a custom shop will admit, and it is the answer we give too when it is true.
